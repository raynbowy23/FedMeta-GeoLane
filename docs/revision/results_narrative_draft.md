**Evaluation protocol.** All results are reported as means with standard deviations over three training seeds. Geometric quantities are expressed in meters and computed identically for every method, and no reported metric contains any learned weighting term. The primary reference is a set of human lane annotations at twelve sites, used for evaluation only. Detected lanes and annotations are projected through the same site homography, so calibration error cancels in the comparison. A site enters the train and test split when its detection error under the fixed configuration is below 5 m. Two sites fail this rule because their ground control points lie near the horizon, and we retain them as calibration case studies.

**Accuracy against the map.** The detected geometry is substantially closer to the annotations than the OpenStreetMap reference that supervises training. Across the twelve annotated sites the mean detection error is 2.9 m, while the offset between the reference lanes and the annotations ranges from 2.6 to 65.9 m with a mean near 26 m. The system therefore recovers lane geometry roughly an order of magnitude more accurate than the public map it learns from, without any human label in the loop. When the reference itself is scored as a detector under the same protocol it reaches an F1 of 0.63 on the training sites and only 0.31 at held out sites, which quantifies the staleness that any behavior grounded method must exceed, and every evaluated method exceeds it at held out locations.

**Adaptation against the fixed configuration.** The scene adaptive models improve on the fixed configuration on every axis that traffic evidence supports. Lane level F1 at the 5 m threshold reaches 0.756 for both adaptive models against 0.698 for the fixed baseline, and the gain comes from recall, 68 against 60 percent of annotated lanes. The clearest effect is lane counting. The fixed configuration is correct on average but fails at particular sites, with count errors of four and five lanes at two locations, while the adaptive models remain near one lane at every site and the mean absolute error halves from 2.33 to 1.06. For a digital twin whose network is instantiated from these counts, reliability at each site matters more than the average across sites.

**External baselines.** The comparison includes two published trajectory based methods run on identical detections, so the differences measure lane learning rather than detection quality. The multiple ROI system of Qiu et al. [28], using the authors' implementation, is competitive on clean highway views and reaches 0.80 at one held out interstate site, while it returns no lanes at two arterial sites whose geometry violates its ROI assumptions, for an overall F1 of 0.52 on training sites and 0.22 at held out sites. The clustering core of Ren et al. [29], in our implementation with thresholds fixed before evaluation, is a strong baseline on raw centerline recall and reaches 0.72 and 0.68, at uniformly lower precision than our methods and without the lane widths, boundaries, or network anchoring that simulation requires. Table [N] therefore separates what each method produces from how well it produces it. Only the proposed system yields the complete anchored lane model that the digital twin consumes, and the strength of simple trajectory clustering on the centerline subtask identifies the separation stage as a natural target for future work.

**Federated against per camera adaptation.** The per camera meta models and the federated model reach the same accuracy. Their F1 values coincide on the training sites and differ within seed noise at held out sites, which is the outcome the federated design aims for, since federated models typically concede accuracy to locally personalized ones under heterogeneous clients. The federated model delivers this parity with one global model in place of a model per camera, with no local training before deployment at a new site, and with 0.9 KB of communication per camera and round against the megabytes per camera that centralizing trajectories would require. Deployment at held out sites is also more repeatable. Across seeds the federated result varies by 0.16 m while nearest site transfer of a per camera model varies by 4.0 m, because transfer quality depends on which donor site the selection happens to pick.

**Robustness.** The volume sweep bounds the traffic dependence raised in review. F1 remains within 0.03 of the full data value when training uses one tenth of the recorded trajectories, so detection quality is limited by lanes that receive no traffic within the observation period rather than by traffic volume. Generalization extends across corridors, since two held out sites lie on a different interstate from every training site.

**Closed loop update.** A lane closure scenario demonstrates the loop from detection to simulation. A closure synthesized in the input data, and disclosed as such, is flagged by comparing detected lane sets across observation windows, mapped to its network lane, applied to the running simulation through TraCI without re importing the network, and re simulated under identical demand, where it raises the mean departure delay by six percent.

**Limitation.** The honest limitation is recall. The methods recover 53 to 68 percent of annotated lanes, and the lane by lane analysis attributes the remainder primarily to lanes with little or no trajectory evidence, which no parameter setting recovers and which longer accumulation addresses.
